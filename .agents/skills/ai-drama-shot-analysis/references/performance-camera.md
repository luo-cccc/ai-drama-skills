# Performance And Camera Guidance

Convert emotional function into visible and audible behavior. Select only dimensions that materially express the beat:

1. Gaze and micro-expression.
2. Posture, weight, and balance.
3. Incomplete or interrupted action.
4. Breath, voice, and physiological response.
5. Movement through motivated light and shadow.
6. Contact, weight, friction, and inertia when physical interaction exists.

Do not impose a quota on every shot. Avoid mechanically repeating eye tremors, lip pressing, clenched fists, or backward steps.

Use camera language at information release, choice, reaction, spatial change, and visual payoff. Establish space before relying on close coverage. Preserve the 180-degree axis, gaze direction, action direction, and object hand unless a visible transition motivates the change.

Choose movement for narrative function, not generic energy. Keep close detail stable enough to read; let wider movement preserve geography. Treat hard cut, match cut, sound bridge, handheld motion, and visual resets as options rather than mandatory patterns.

Light must come from established sources. Physical feedback must respect support, collision, weight, and momentum. Do not use unexplained light shifts or floating props as emotional shorthand.
