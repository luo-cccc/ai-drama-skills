# Character Design Modes

## Comprehensive

Create a wide production sheet containing: character portrait; full-body front, character-left profile, and back; hair structure; face close-ups; garment construction details; material swatches; short Chinese annotations. Align turnaround head, eyes, shoulders, waist, knees, and soles. Use a neutral studio background and even light. Do not add expressions, alternate costumes, story action, or scenery unless requested.

## Orthographic

Use true orthographic or long-lens low-distortion views with identical scale and a neutral A-pose.

- Three views: Front, Left, Back.
- Four views, default: Front, Left, Right, Back.
- Six views: add Top and Bottom only when requested.

Define Left and Right from the character's perspective. Do not mirror asymmetric hair, scars, jewelry, armor, fasteners, holsters, or patterns. Align body landmarks and sole baseline. Keep limbs separated enough to read seams and silhouette. Move held weapons to an auxiliary asset panel. Use a plain background, no perspective floor, dramatic shadow, depth blur, or story pose.

## Expression

Use a fixed head angle, crop, lens, lighting, hairstyle, clothing neckline, and grid. Default to 12 cells when the user does not specify a set: neutral, slight smile, joy, sadness, anger, fear, surprise, doubt, embarrassment, indifference, crying, and laughter.

Make emotion readable through brows, eyelids, gaze, cheeks, nostrils, lips, jaw, and controlled head tension. Preserve skull, feature spacing, skin marks, age, and styling. Avoid identity-changing caricature. Do not duplicate one face with label-only changes.

## Pose

Use full-body cells with consistent identity, costume, scale, and camera. Prefer story-relevant actions. If the user requests a generic library, use up to 15: stand, walk, run, sit, kneel, draw, turn, look up, look down, wave, attack, defend, cast/use ability, jump, interact.

Show a distinct line of action, center of gravity, contact, counterbalance, joint range, cloth response, and prop interaction. Keep transitional poses physically plausible. Do not invent a weapon or ability; omit or mark it `unknown` when undefined.

## Costume

Show the same character and one `COSTUME-NNN` in front/side/back, then construction breakdowns and close details. Separate visible layers and parts: base layer, outer layer, collar, sleeves, closure, waist, hem, footwear, armor, accessories, patterns, seams, and fasteners. Include material and color references that exist in the design.

For reference-driven work, distinguish visible, partially visible, hidden, and absent components. Extend hidden construction conservatively; do not add embroidery, hardware, openings, or decoration without evidence. A new costume version must retain the character's immutable identity.

## Prop

Inventory the character's significant wearable and handled props. Use the established `PROP-NNN`; separate crowded sets into sequential plates. For each prop show an overall three-quarter view, required orthographic views, functional parts, attachment or wearing location, scale against the character or known object, materials, colors, fabrication, and use wear.

Preserve count, handedness, side placement, attachment, and size. Do not invent internals, functions, symbols, or decoration. Do not show a hand unless grip or ergonomic evidence is specifically required; then use a neutral unidentifiable mannequin hand.
